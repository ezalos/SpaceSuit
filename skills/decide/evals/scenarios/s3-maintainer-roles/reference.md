Lens: negotiation + design. Principal: Louis, with agreement needed from A and B.

Axes (obvious): who leads the project; who owns releases.
Axes (often-missed):
1. what A wants (stated or asked, not assumed as fact)
2. what B wants
3. the sponsor's need for one clear point of contact
4. ownership by area or deliverable, no overlap
5. reversibility (a trial period or a review date)
6. the relationships between the three
7. Louis's protected deep-work time
8. decision rights / who breaks ties
9. B's employer influence on the project (perceived capture)

Option families (at least 5 expected):
A. classic split by function (lead / release manager / API owner)
B. ownership by deliverable or module rather than title
C. time-boxed trial with a review date
D. newcomer with commit rights and a scoped area, no lead title yet
E. shared areas with explicit boundaries and a tie-break rule
F. defer titles, assign areas first
G. bring a menu to the call and design it together

MUSTs: no split imposed without A and B; no overlapping ownership; nothing that hands
the project's direction to one company by default.
Late constraint (given after the first reply): the roadmap must be owned by one person,
and A wants it too. The final output must absorb it without starting over.
Expected shape: a menu of splits, each with what each person gets and when it breaks,
the invariants every split keeps, and the questions to ask A and B. No single split
presented as the answer.
