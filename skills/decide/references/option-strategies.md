# Option search

Each sub-agent gets **one** brief below, the record path, and this common preamble:

> Read the decision record at `<path>`. Generate options for it using only the strategy below.
> Return 3–6 options, each as: a name, one sentence on what it is, and the axes it wins and
> loses on. Options must respect every item under `## Never`. Do not evaluate, rank or
> recommend. Web searches: search for the general fact (typical ranges, how a tool behaves),
> never for names or details from the record.

Pick at least four briefs that fit the lens. In design and negotiation decisions, use
*combine* and *their side* every time.

## Briefs

1. **Extremes.** For two or three axes in turn, design the option that maxes that axis and
   accepts losing elsewhere. Extremes mark the edges of the frontier, and the good
   compromises sit between them.
2. **Combine.** Build a strategy table: one column per sub-decision (what, who, when, how
   much, which terms, and in what order the people involved hear it). Pick 3–4 coherent rows across the columns, each with a theme.
3. **Wait, buy time, do nothing.** Delay, ask for an extension, run a small trial first, stage
   the commitment, keep the status quo. Include the cost of waiting.
4. **Reframe.** Change who decides, what is decided, when, or the unit of the decision
   (a trial instead of a commitment, ownership by deliverable instead of by title, a break
   clause instead of a shorter term), or get the same result from a different source or
   provider.
5. **Borrow.** How would another field or an expert solve this (procurement, diplomacy, ops,
   a seasoned engineer)? Port their standard move.
6. **Their side** (negotiation lens). Design packages around the other party's likely
   interests, so that it costs Louis little and is worth a lot to them. Where they want the
   same thing he does, each package says what they keep of it. Offering several equivalent
   packages at once (MESOs) both gives them a choice and shows what they value.
7. **Middle ground.** Find the options between the obvious two: the intermediate term, the
   capped version, the hybrid. A baseline agent misses these most often.
8. **Partition** (when something is shared between people: roles, ownership, scope). Cut it
   different ways: by deliverable or artifact, by time horizon, by decision rights, by
   rotation, by a trial period with a review date.

## Merge

1. Deduplicate: two options are the same if they win and lose on the same axes for the same
   reason. Keep the clearer name.
2. Group the survivors into **families**, by the main idea, not the details.
3. **Diversity check.** Fewer than five families in full mode (three in quick mode), or one
   family holding more than half of the options: dispatch the briefs not yet used, once (in
   quick mode, apply them inline yourself).
   Then go on with what you have.
